Analysis Insight
⚠

DTC window dominated by background VehicleSpeed anomalies

Both P0562 and P0117 fire on BMS / EMS but the DTC-window scoring surfaces TCU / VehicleSpeed as the top candidate. This is because VehicleSpeed generates persistent rule violations throughout the entire 60 s run (443 events), flooding any ±2 s window. The true causal spike — large BatteryVoltage (56.5) and CoolantTemp (55.6) scores — occurs at t = 40.1 s, which is 6–10 s after the DTCs fire. Consider tightening the DTC window, adding ECU-ownership weighting, or filtering out baseline-level VehicleSpeed violations before scoring.

✓

Strong drive-cluster correlation confirms sensor health

EngineSpeed, ThrottlePosition and VehicleSpeed form a tight cluster (r ≥ 0.979, lag = 0). This suggests the drive-train sensing chain is intact and the MDF resampling alignment is correct. The BatteryVoltage ↔ CoolantTemp inverse correlation (−0.955) is physically plausible: as the engine warms up, charging system voltage dips slightly.

→

Recommended next step — autoencoder anomaly detection

The rule-based detector flags 443 / 454 events on VehicleSpeed alone, suggesting the rule threshold (max 250 km/h) is too broad for this dataset. Training an LSTM autoencoder on the normal_run_01 / normal_run_02 reference data will learn the natural drive-cluster covariance and produce residual-based anomaly scores that are inherently self-normalising and cluster-aware — directly addressing the DTC-window false-ranking problem.
